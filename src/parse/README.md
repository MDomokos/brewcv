# src/parse

Turns the text of a resume file into JSON Resume. The readers below turn each format into the text items the parser takes; a LinkedIn export skips the parser. It is a port of the parser in [OpenResume](https://github.com/xitanggg/open-resume) (`src/app/lib/parse-resume-from-pdf/`, commit `4f8255a2c763479837f69f1dccf2a3338730cd79`), plain JavaScript, types stripped, by Xitang Zhao in the original. Each ported file says where it came from. It imports nothing from the rest of `src/`, so it can be split out with `git subtree split --prefix=src/parse`.

```js
import { parseResume } from './index.js';
const { jsonResume, trace, leftovers, layout, doubts } = parseResume(textItems, lexicon, layout?);
```

**In: text items**, in the order a reader found them, one line ended by `hasEOL`:
`{ text, x, y, width, height, fontName, hasEOL, page?, title?, side?, caps?, underline?, color?, bottom?, wrap? }`. A bold item has `Bold` in its `fontName`; `height` is the font size in points; `caps` (set in capitals by its style), `underline`, `color` (hex, none for the text colour) and `link` (a hyperlink) are there when the file says so; `bottom` (the y where the last of several joined lines sat) and `wrap` (the gaps between them) are there on a PDF line the reader joined. `y` grows upward, as in a PDF. `title: true` is a heading the reader found (a Heading style, `#`, `h1` to `h3`) at the top level the document uses after its first two lines: a section title whatever its words, unless it is in the first two lines.

**In: a lexicon** (optional): the words to look for, in `lexicon.js` form. Leave it out for English. The words you give are added to the English ones, except `months`, which replaces them (twelve names, January first). `lexiconFor(lang)` in `src/model/import.js` builds one from the app's catalogues.

**In: a layout** (optional), one entry per page, `[{ page, headerBottom?, split? }]`: lines whose top (`y + height`) is above `headerBottom` are a full-width header, read first; the rest are cut at x = `split`, left column first. No `split` is one column. A page you name is used as it is; a page you leave out is guessed, and the guess splits only when sure (a gap of 30 pt or more between line starts, three lines on each side, rows that meet, few lines across the cut) and otherwise reads one column.

**Out**
- `jsonResume`: `basics`, `work`, `education`, `projects`, `skills`, with dates as `YYYY-MM` or `YYYY`. Education bullets go in `courses`. A skills line `Group: a, b` is a group named Group.
- `trace`: `[{ path, page?, line }]`, for each text in `jsonResume`, the page and the line (counted from 1, after the lines are grouped) of the first line that holds it.
- `layout`: what was used for every page, with the guessed `headerBottom` and `split`, so a screen can draw it and the person can move it.
- `doubts`: what to check by eye: `{ kind: 'columns', page }` (a column was seen but not trusted, or trusted with a question), `{ kind: 'leftovers', n }`, `{ kind: 'dates', n }` (jobs with no dates), `{ kind: 'company', n }` (jobs with no company: the dated line held none, or only a place), `{ kind: 'name' }`, `{ kind: 'jobs' }` (no job found in a file of more than ten lines: the file was probably not laid out with headings, so lines need placing by hand), `{ kind: 'letter' }` (the file reads like a cover letter: at most one section title (a bold subject line in capitals), at least three prose lines of eight words or more holding most of the text, and not dated: no date range with a year near its dash, and no more than one line with a year; no `jobs` doubt is raised for it).
- `leftovers`: `[{ text, section, page?, line }]`, the lines that fall in a section but that no extractor used, and the lines of items marked `side: 'header'` or `'footer'` (a Word file's running header and footer; they are not read, they have no `line`, and the header's come first, the footer's last). The title of a section is not a leftover; the lines under a section nothing reads are. A paragraph that wraps over lines is one leftover (a line joins the leftover above it when that has six words or more and the gap is one line); a block under the work heading with no job line above its text (a paragraph, a bullet) is no job, so its lines come back as leftovers.

**What changed from OpenResume**: a column split before the lines are grouped (`split-columns.js`: two columns are trusted when their rows overlap by half a line, even with no shared baseline, and at least half of the right side's lines start at the cut, so dates set right within a column are no column); the word lists are a lexicon; any Unicode letter counts as a letter; the output is JSON Resume with a trace and leftovers (`to-json-resume.js`); for PDFs, line gaps measured from the bottom of a joined line and across a page break, a typical letter width when no item is in both the commonest size and font, a separator is not a word, and an entry never opens with a description or ends in a bare title (`subsections.js`); the lines under a contact heading are the profile's too, a phone with a plus, "City, Country" and a bare domain are found (`extract-profile.js`); a title line and a line of company, dates and place give the company and the position either way round (a first line larger than the dated line is the title, else the company; a part on the dated line before or after a comma next to the dates counts; when nothing decides, both are kept and `company` is raised; a second dated line in a job is a second role at the same company, never glued into the first's title; a line of "Company · Title" with no date is cut in two; a header in smaller print ends where the text's font changes) (`extract-work-experience.js`); bold capitals with a digit ("CAD & 3D") are no section title, and patent, publication, language, certificate, interest, reference, volunteer and contact words are. A short bold line that ends in a section word is a title even as the first line of the page (a column that starts with its heading); the lines around an address in another column (a sidebar's name and contact block, read after the main column) are the profile's; every section with a work word counts as the work ("Experience" and "Earlier work"); a header in a monospace font ends where the text's font changes; with no job-title word, the line above a lone date line is the title and the line below it the company. `read-pdf.ts` is not ported as it was: `pdf.js` is its adapter. `types.ts` is types only.

**Readers** (all but `identify` and `unzip` give text items; each takes the file's bytes; `identify` throws unzip's coded error for a ZIP it cannot list):
- `identify(bytes, name)`: `pdf | docx | doc | odt | pages | linkedin | html | md | txt | json | image | other` (`doc` is an old binary Word file: an OLE file that holds the stream name `WordDocument`; a Pages file is told by its preview PDF or its `Index/Document.iwa`, and a Keynote file, which holds `Index/Slide*.iwa`, is `other`; a Numbers file reads as Pages), from the bytes; the name only breaks the tie between `md` and `txt`. `unzip(bytes)`: `{ names, read(name) }` over the platform's own inflate; refuses ZIP64, encrypted files and more than 64 MB read from one file in all, with an error that has a `code`. The readers scan tags in one pass (`scan` in `lines.js`), so broken markup cannot make them slow; a text box, frame or footnote inside a paragraph never splits it (boxes are read as lines after it). A DOCX's header and footer (the ones its last section uses: default, or first-page when that is all there is) come back as `side` items, minus page numbers and lines the body has too, so the parser leaves them as leftovers; a DOCX, ODT or page with no paragraph in it is refused with the code `empty`.
- `readDocx`, `readOdt`, `readHtml`, `readMd`, `readTxt`: one item per part of a line, one line per paragraph, list item or heading, so the same resume in any of them gives the same lines. They have no page positions: every line starts at x = 0 and `y` is minus the line number, so the parser never guesses columns for them and reports no column doubt. A tab or two spaces start a new item on the line (a date at the right), but a gap right after `.`, `!` or `?` never does (sentence spacing), so "Acme Inc.  2019 – 2021" stays one item; an empty paragraph is a gap in `y`, which is how the parser tells one job from the next.
- `readPdf(bytes, words?)` (`pdf.js`) gives the items of a PDF, with the vendored PDF.js (its legacy build) (`vendor/pdfjs/`, loaded by `import()` the first time, a worker on the page's own address). Items carry `page` (and `y` starts again on each page); `fontName` is the real font name, with `-Bold` added when the font is bold. A PDF keeps the order its producer wrote, with these repairs: a space PDF.js made from a gap is part of the text again (and is dropped before `,` `.` `;` `:` `!` `?`); a bullet of any shape is `•`; letter-spaced capitals are one word (also when the font kerned some pairs: "TA PA S Z TA L AT"); a page written in a jumbled order is put top to bottom; a label in the left margin beside a line (a section name set in a gutter, a tagline) is a line of its own at the text's edge, so the parser sees no column; and the lines of a paragraph or bullet that the page wrapped are one line again (so a PDF and the DOCX of one resume give the same lines and `sameDocument` links them). A joined line carries `bottom` (where its last line sat) and `wrap` (the gaps inside it) for the parser's line gaps. A word cut at the line end is joined when its hyphen is a soft hyphen or a hyphen of its own (a browser's), or when the whole word is in the file or in `words` (the lowercase words the caller knows, the record's); any other hyphen is a compound and stays. A password-protected file is refused with the code `encrypted`, any other that cannot be read (or that PDF.js fails on in a page) with `pdf`, and more than 30 pages, 10,000 text items on a page or 30,000 in all with `big`; the worker is ended after each read. Lines are joined only when both are all bold or neither (a bold heading is not the next line of a sentence), and not when the line above is short for its column and the next starts with a capital; in a column under 22 ems wide a capital opens a new item unless the line above runs to the edge or ends with a comma, and a line that ends a sentence or a year is never joined. `pdfThumb(bytes, width?)` draws page 1 on a canvas and returns a PNG data URL (browser only; the code `big` for a page it cannot draw); `pdfPages(bytes, width?, max?)` draws the first 8 pages the same way, each as `{ url, width, height, pw, ph }` (pixels and the page's own points), for the layout view.
- `readPages(bytes)` gives the PDF a Pages file holds, for `readPdf`; a modern Pages file holds only a picture, and is refused with the code `pages-preview`. `readLinkedin(bytes)` gives `{ jsonResume, trace, leftovers, doubts }` from the CSV files of a LinkedIn export (the last three empty). `parseCsv` is in `csv.js`.
- The text of every item is mended, not rewritten (`lines.js`): ligatures, soft and zero-width characters, odd spaces. Quotes, dashes, capitals and the spacing around punctuation stay as the person typed them (a PDF's own damage, such as a word split at a line end, is for the PDF reader); `normText` smooths them for matching only.
- **Documents formatted by hand** (no heading styles): the readers pass on the look of the text, not only its words. Word follows a run's size, colour, underline and caps through its character style, its paragraph style (and the styles those are based on) and the document defaults; ODT reads them from the styles in `content.xml`; HTML from inline `style=""` (size in pt or px, weight, colour, underline, upper case) and `<u>`. A line that opens with a typed bullet ("•", "-", "▪", "*", "o" and a tab, an en dash when three lines of the document open with one, or a symbol-font bullet: U+F0B7, F0A7, F0D8, F0FC, F076, also from `w:sym`) is a list item; any other symbol alone in front of a line is dropped; a line break inside a paragraph starts a new line; a line of spaced capitals of five letters or more ("E X P E R I E N C E") is read as its word; a Word or ODT table of two rows or fewer, with no merged cell and a cell of four lines or more (a sidebar layout) is read column by column, any other table row by row. The parser takes a short line alone on its row (four words at most, no digits, no job-title word) as a section title when its look is the titles' look: larger than the body text (1.5 pt, and the more the higher), underlined, coloured or bold unlike it. The titles' look is that of the titles it already finds by their words, and then the line needs a title word at its end ("Professional Work Experience"; "Workday" and "Art History Museum" are no titles); with no such title it is the most emphasised look that two lines or more have. Links, profile-site words and the contact block never set the look. It is used only when no reader marked a heading, and only for ten lines or fewer.
- A heading is bold, and a section title only at the top heading level the document uses (the smallest number, after the first two lines), so Heading 2 job lines under Heading 1 sections stay jobs.
